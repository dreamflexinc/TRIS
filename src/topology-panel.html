    What the geometry and measurements mean

    The live graph

    Nodes represent TRIScore application modules and their instrumented subengines in this browser, not native operating-system processes. Solid signal edges come from accepted Resonance routes. Dashed edges are declared transfer paths or configured Kaos couplings, not proof of a completed transfer. Event pulses report observed navigation, control requests, or app events. No journal, password, or Tris memory text is inspected.

    The component count and cycle rank describe the current filtered graph after directions and duplicate pairs are removed. Display motion does not measure an OS-wide Lyapunov exponent.

    The attractor & sensitivity

    The reference flow integrates the Lorenz equations (σ=10, ρ=28, β=8/3) with RK4 at Δt=0.005. A tangent vector is integrated and renormalized each step; its logarithmic growth is averaged after 10 model-time units of burn-in. A 30-unit warm start provides the initial trace. λ̂ belongs to this reference model. Nexus and Frontier estimates appear on their own node when available.

    Select Live Resonance Lorenz to trace the coupled engine's actual coordinates at up to five observations per second, retaining 1,000 points. Dock the monitor while running Resonance. No Lyapunov rate is inferred from those samples. Node placement along either path is a visual mapping; lab equations and timesteps stay in their own engines.

    Calabi–Yau geometry

    The Fermat quintic is Z₀⁵+Z₁⁵+Z₂⁵+Z₃⁵+Z₄⁵=0 in CP⁴. Here Z₀=1 and Z₃=Z₄=0, leaving 1+z₁⁵+z₂⁵=0. We sample five root sheets of that real two-dimensional slice and project four real coordinates into three. Gaps at chart branch cuts are omitted mesh connections, not boundaries of the whole manifold.

    The optional harmonic loops solve a periodic one-dimensional wave equation before an illustrative embedding into the slice. No Ricci-flat metric, Nambu–Goto evolution, compactification dynamics, or physical string-theory prediction is computed.

    The atomic lens

    Positions come directly from the accepted Matter 3D checkpoint. Edges show candidate neighbors inside its global cutoff, plus declared harmonic dimer bonds. They are not inferred chemical bonds or a per-pair force measurement; species-specific interactions may differ. At most 128 particles and 1,200 edges are shown, with truncation reported. Neighbor distances use periodic minimum images; line positions use the wrapped display box.

    Snapshots are explicit, unencrypted telemetry exports. Browser storage and Tris vaults are not changed by observing. Sentinel sealing clears recent observations and pauses this view.

    Exports contain graph metadata and numerical observations only.
